## Failure Mechanism

1. The program recomputed a SINGLE joint-space move from the observation pose
   to the final alignment pose. It did not first follow the previous pregrasp
   route and then append a constrained local approach.
2. Removing 0.40 m of standoff and reducing the tool offset by 0.05 m moved the
   requested flange endpoint about 0.45 m farther toward the target, apart from
   small new-detection differences. The different endpoint IK substantially
   changed J1/J4 travel and every intermediate joint-interpolation pose.
3. Joint interpolation is not Cartesian position/orientation interpolation.
   A smooth, valid joint trajectory can swing the tool laterally. Low speed
   changes timing, not the geometric route or collision possibility.
4. Checks covered limits, flange workspace, FK/Jacobian conditioning, actual
   joint-path agreement and the grasp-center signed distance from one target
   plane. They did NOT model the two fingers, palm, flange, cables or robot
   links against the mannequin, the other arm or the environment.
5. A point remaining outside the target plane cannot establish clearance of
   the entire tool against other body parts. Matching the planned trajectory
   cannot detect a collision built into that plan. The endpoint orientation
   bound also was not a bound on the entire swept tool geometry.

The supported conclusion is a planning/safety-validation failure, not proof
of a controller malfunction, a coordinate-transform error or an unexpected
IK-branch jump during execution. A full mechanical reconstruction needs data
not present in the progress-only log.

## Containment and Required Redesign

- Python rejects `--grasp-align --execute`; C++ independently rejects both
  `--joint-grasp-align ... --execute` and `--linear-grasp-align ... --execute`
  before file loading/robot connection. Planning-only modes remain available.
- Existing 40 cm pregrasp success is NOT a general collision guarantee and
  must not be used as a workaround to resume this task after the incident.
- Keep the explicit 300 mm measurement; do not tune TCP length to hide a route
  problem. Check mounting/calibration after any contact that may have moved it.
- Plan a collision-checked transit to a pregrasp pose, followed by a constrained
  local insertion direction with bounded lateral drift and orientation change.
  Prefer staged approach over one unconstrained long joint move to the grasp.
- Validate actual finger/link swept volumes against the scene. Without that
  validation, do not represent sample counts as clearance or enable automatic
  zero-standoff execution. A staged route alone is not a collision certificate.
- Onsite recovery must consider the actual contact and E-stop state; do not
  automatically return along a historical or reversed trajectory.
